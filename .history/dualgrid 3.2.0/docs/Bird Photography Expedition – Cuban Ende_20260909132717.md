Overview

The Bird Photography Expedition (10 Days) is a specialized program crafted for photographers and birding enthusiasts who want to capture Cuba’s most iconic endemic species and Caribbean specialties through the lens. From the limestone mogotes of Viñales Valley and the forests of Las Terrazas, to the wetlands of the Zapata Swamp and the cultural landscapes of Trinidad, this tour offers unparalleled opportunities to photograph Cuba’s avian treasures in diverse habitats.

The program balances endemic treasures with Caribbean specialties, giving photographers access to 26 Cuban endemics and 17 West Indies endemics that call Cuba home. Sessions are scheduled in reserves and gardens where birds are approachable, ensuring close encounters and natural compositions. Alongside the birding, you’ll experience Cuba’s cultural richness — from the limestone mogotes of Viñales Valley to  Zapata Swamp and colonial streets of Trinidad.

This is not just a birding tour; it’s a photographic journey that combines technical opportunities with authentic Cuban landscapes and culture, making it the ideal choice for travelers who want to return home with both memories and images of Cuba’s avian wonders.

DAY 1. Arrival.

Welcome to Cuba! Upon arrival to Jose Marti International Airport (HAV) in Havana, you will be met by your guide. Transfer to Vinales (3h) with birding stops along the way.
Overnight in Vinales 2 night. Lodging and dinner in Hostal.
Viñales is a small town with an economy based on agriculture, mainly tobacco farming, and its soil is considered to be some of the richest in the world. During these two days in the Viñales Valley, a UNESCO World Heritage site, you’ll see mountainous “mogotes”, made of hard limestone left over after millennia of erosion underwater and now were emerged.

DAY 2. Vinales

Breakfast in hostal.
Morning: Departure to the trail "Maravillas de Viñales", about 30 minutes from the town. This in an exceptional place to get photographs of the birds because there is special conditions created to this activity. An area rich in endemic birds such as Cuban Tody, Cuban Bullfinch, Cuban Grassquit, Cuban Trogon, Yellow-headed Warbler, Cuban Green Woodpecker and the Cuban Solitaire. And many other resident species such as Great Lizard-Cuckoo, the Red-legged Honeycreeper, the Cuban Emerald, and the Olive-capped Warbler. You can get too, many winter migratory as diverse species of Warblers, Tanagers and Orioles. In addition to the possibility of observing and photographing various species of mollusks and reptile’s endemic to Cuba, the Anolis vermiculatus, the only aquatic habitat lizard in the Caribbean.

Lunch in local Restaurant Las Palmitas in Ancon Valley, buffet style.

Afternoon: Visit “La Costanera” pine forest. Some of the specialities to be found in this area are Gundlach's Hawk, Northern (Cuban) Flicker, Yellow-headed Warbler, Cuban Bullfinch, Cuban Grassquit, Olive-capped Warbler, a species found only in Bahamas and Cuba, and the superb singer, the Cuban Solitaire.

Dinner in Restaurant Buenavista in Mirador Los Jazmines.

In Viñales we may visit Nils Navarro’s house. Nils is one of the most important ornithologist in Cuba at this time, and author of the Endemics Birds of Cuba: A Comprehensive Guide. Illustrated with his own watercolors. Author too of The Checklist of the Birds of Cuba, a periodical edition updated each year with many useful information and every new record of birds to Cuba. And now is working in a new book, The Guide of Birds of Cuba.
Dinner in Finca Paraiso at sunset.

Overnight in Viñales

DAYS 3. Las Terrazas

Early departure from Vinales to Las Terrazas Community ( 2h). Las Terrazas is a small rural tourist community of sustainable development that offers you a unique environment in connecting with nature. Located 75 kilometers west of Havana, this place is part of the Sierra del Rosario, cataloged by UNESCO as a Biosphere Reserve in 1985.  We can find endemics as Cuban Trogon, Cuban Bullfinch, Cuban Tody, specially the endangered Cuban Grassquit, and other endemics such as Cuban Pygmy Owl, Cuban Oriole, and the parasitic Shiny Cowbird.

Lunch would be held in Finca Rocio Ecofarm, buffet style,  a quite farm with lots of birds and the chance of learning about apiculture, they have dozens of Stingless bee hives, that are endemic to the continent.  The Las Terrazas project arose at the end of the sixties with the purpose of recovering an area that had been destroyed by the intensive cultivation of coffee in the 19th century, so much so that, in an area of 5000 hectares, millions of trees were planted, terraces were dug for cultivation and a small town was built following guidelines that respect nature.

After lunch continue the road to Soroa.  Visit the Orchidarium. Soroa Orchid Garden in the Sierra del Rosario Biosphere Reserve is a prime birdwatching destination in western Cuba, combining lush tropical vegetation with a rich avian diversity. The garden’s humid, mountainous setting and abundant flowering plants attract a wide range of birds, from endemic Cuban species to rare migrant warblers ( Cuban Trogon, Cuban Green Woodpecker, Cuban Oriole, Cuban Emerald, Scaly-naped Pigeon )

Check in Hostal Soroa for 1 night.

DAY 4. Road to Zapata,

Breakfast. Early departure to Escarleras de Jaruco (2h), with scenic views and karst slopes. We stop at Mirador del Árabe, where sweeping views set the stage for sightings of the Cuban Solitaire, the park’s signature species, famous for its haunting, flute-like song. This blend of dramatic landscapes and endemic species makes Escaleras de Jaruco a highlight. Continue to Zapata Peninsula (3+ hours).
Lunch at KM139 for a delicious Cuban Sandwich with a Fresh Fruit Mislkshake.
Arrival to Playa larga, followed by a visit to Casa Ana Birding, best place to see the Bee Hummingbird (world’s smallest bird). At this backyard you would encounter also Cuban Emeralds, Cuban Trogon, Cuban Pigmy Owl, Tawny-shouldered Blackbirds, Yellow-Faced Grassquits, as well as many migratory warblers ( Yellow-throated Warbler, Cape-May Warbler, Black-throated Blue Warbler, and Palm Warblers).
Arrive to Playa Larga.
Accomodation in Bnb Playa Larga for 4 nights. Dinner.
Night: Brief departure (walking) to the neighborhood of Caletón in Playa Larga with the objective of observing, if possible, the Sygian Owl (Asio stygius) in the vicinity of the beach. A highly elusive bird and always interesting to observe.

DAY 5. Zapata Swamp .

Breakfast in your Casa Particular (very early this time)
Morning: Early departure to the town of “Santo Tomás”, an area of forests and grasslands of swamp. Habitat of two of the most restricted bird species in Cuba, the Zapata Wren and Zapata Sparrow. We will use boats to navigate through “La Cocodrila” channel, in a deep swamp area. Having the possibility of observing and photographing also different endemic species such as the Gray-fronted Quail-dove, Red-shouldered Blackbird, Cuban Vireo, Cuban Pygmy-Owl, Cuban Trogon, Cuban Tody, Cuban Blackbird, Yellow-headed Warbler and Cuban Green Woodpecker. As well as Cuban residents such as Cuban Crow, Western Spindalis, Scaly-naped Pigeon, White-crowned Pigeon, Great Lizard-Cuckoo and numerous resident and migratory species.
Lunch .
Afternoon: After lunch we can use a couple of hours to visit “Caminos al Vuelo” garden. Aother wonderful space to keep snapping pictures of the Bee Humminbird. where a local guide keeps a garden  with excellent condition to observe and photographing the Bee Hummingbird, world’s smallest bird, and many other endemics and resident birds.

Dinner. Overnight in casa particular.

Night: Brief departure (walking) to the neighborhood of Caletón in Playa Larga with the objective of observing, if possible, the Sygian Owl (Asio stygius) in the vicinity of the beach. A highly elusive bird and always interesting to observe.


DAY 6. Zapata Swamp

Breakfast
Morning: Departure to “Salinas de Brito” a very good place to see some endemics like Cuban Black Hawk.  This area has a road that cross through marshes, lagoons, mangroves and forest. Where is possible find many birds such as the White Ibis, different ducks species, Black skimmers, yellowlegs, Pelicans, Roseate spoonbills, and numerous migratory species as the Osprey, gulls and terns. There is a place with a healthy population of American Falmingo.

Lunch.

Free time to return to Bee hummingbird Garden or resting.

Over 4pm departure to the town of Soplillar. Area of forests and savannahs. One of the sites with better possibilities to observe the rare Fernandina´s Flicker, endemic to Cuba. Having the possibility of observing and photographing other endemic species such as the Cuban Parakeet, , the Cuban Pygmy-Owl, Bare-legged Owl, the Cuban Trogon, the Cuban Tody, the Cuban Blackbird, the Cuban Green Woodpecker and the Yellow-headed Warbler. As well as others cuban residents such as the Cuban Parrot, Key West Quail-dove, Cuban Crow, Western Spindalis, Great Lizard-Cuckoo and numerous others resident and migratory species. In the sunset is possible find a Cuban Nightjar very close to a cow farm on this area.

Dinner. Overnight in casa particular.

DAY 7. Zapata Swamp
Morning: Departure before sunrise towards the "Bermejas’s Fauna Refuge". Considered one of the best birdwatching areas in Cuba, with one of the highest concentrations of endemics in the entire country. It is an area of forests where you can observe and photograph endemics such as Blue-headed Quail-dove, Gray-fronted Quail-dove, Cuban Parakeet, Cuban Pygmy-Owl, Bare-legged Owl, Fernandina´s Flicker, Cuban Trogon, Cuban Tody, Red-shouldered Blackbird, Yellow-headed Warbler, Cuban Vireo, Cuban Blackbird, Cuban Green Woodpecker and Cuban Oriole. As well other residents such as Key West Quail-dove, Cuban Parrot, Ruddy Quail-dove, Cuban Crow, Cuban Emerald, Western Spindalis and Great Lizard-Cuckoo. And numerous migratory species that use this forest as a winter refuge.
Lunch in Cueva de los Peces restaurant.

Afternoon: "La Cueva de los Peces", a salt water cenote of incredible transparency and 77 meters deep and with the service of snorkeling inside of the cenote or in the coast just in from of this place. In its surroundings it is possible to photograph very close and with an excellent light the beautiful Blue-headed Quail-dove, one of the most striking endemics of Cuba. As well as many other endemic and numerous resident and migratory birds. After this brief stop, we will back to the town of “Los Hondones” an area of deep forests. Having the possibility of observing and photographing other endemic species such as the Cuban Parakeet, the Blue Headed Quail Dove, the Cuban Pygmy-Owl, the Cuban Trogon, the Cuban Tody, the Cuban Blackbird, the Cuban Green Woodpecker  and the Yellow-headed Warbler. As well as others cuban residents such as the Cuban Parrot, Key West Quail-dove,  Western Spindalis, Great Lizard-Cuckoo and numerous others resident and migratory species.

Dinner. Overnight in casa particular.


DAY 8. Cienfuegos

Breakfast and early departure to Cienfuegos city (2h) with stops at Babiney rice fields for waterfowls like King Rail and Glossy Ibis. Visit the Botanical Garden, chances of Gundlach Hawk nesting. Followed by a drive to Guajimico International Villa (1h) for lunch and biridng around the coastal shrubby vegetation, targets are Cuban Tody, Cuban Gnatcatcher, Oriente Warbler. Continue to Trinidad, with more stops on the way. Check in Hostal B&B Trinidad for 1 night.

DAY 9. Trinidad

Breakfast and birding around Trinidad and the scenic Escambray Valley in search of Palm Crow, Cuban Crow, and if previously missed, Giant Kingbird. On the road between Cienfuegos and Trinidad we will make some stops to find an endemic Cuban Gnatcatcher and Oriente Warbler, as well as the hybrids between Yellow-headed Warbler and Oriente Warbler, a phenomenon that occurs only there, the only known contact point of the distribution area of this two endemics species.
Return to Havana (6h+). Lunch.
Arrival to Havana late afternoon.
Check in Hostal Boutique for 1 night.
Farewell Dinner.

DAY 10. Departure

Breakfast and transfer to Jose Marti International Airport IN Havana for your return flight
