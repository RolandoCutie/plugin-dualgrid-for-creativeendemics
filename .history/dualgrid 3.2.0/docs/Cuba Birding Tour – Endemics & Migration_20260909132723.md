OVERVIEW

The Cuba Birding Tour – Endemics & Migration (10 Days) is a mid‑length program designed for birders who want to experience both Cuba’s rare endemics and winter migratory warblers. From the limestone mogotes of Viñales Valley to the wetlands of the Zapata Peninsula, and onward to the pristine keys of Cayo Coco, this itinerary offers unrivaled opportunities to encounter Cuba’s avian treasures across diverse habitats.

This tour blends the thrill of spotting Cuba’s endemic species with the excitement of migratory birds that come from North America. Expect encounters with the Cuban Trogon, Cuban Solitaire, Fernandina’s Flicker, Bee Hummingbird, Zapata Wren, Zapata Sparrow, and Blue‑headed Quail‑Dove, alongside colorful migrants such as warblers, tanagers, and orioles.

Beyond birding, you’ll enjoy cultural touches like a visit to Tobacco farmers and learn about Cuban cigars, the Bay of Pigs Museum, and the historic colonial town of Trinidad. The program also includes time on Cuba’s northern keys, where Caribbean specialties like Bahama Mockingbird, Thick‑billed Vireo, and West‑Indian Whistling Duck await and endmics as Cuban Gnatcatcher, Cuban Black Hawk and Oriente Warbler thrive.



Day 1 ~ Welcome to Cuba

Welcome to Cuba! Upon arrival to Havana, you will be met by your guide.
Depart from Havana with your local guide to Vinales, a small town with an economy based on tourism and agriculture, mainly tobacco farming. During these two days in the Viñales Valley, a UNESCO World Heritage site, you’ll see mountainous “mogotes”, made of hard limestone left over after millennia of erosion underwater and now were emerged. As well as the real Cuban rural farmland views.

On the road we will make stops at Nina Bonita Reservoir and at Los Palacios Fish Ponds for ducks, Snail Kites, and waterbirds in dams built near to the highway.

Accommodation: Guesthouse, Viñales (2 nights)
Meals: Dinner


Day 2 ~ Vinales

Early Breakfast in your hostal. Visit San Vicente Farm, an area rich in endemic birds such as Cuban Trogon, Cuban Green Woodpecker, Cuban Solitaire. And many other resident species such as Great Lizard-Cuckoo, West Indian Woodpecker, Red-legged Honeycreeper, Scaly-naped Pigeon. Departure to “La Costanera” pine forest. Some of the specialities to be found in this area are Yellow headed Warbler, Cuban Vireo, Cuban Bullfinch and Caribbean endemics such as Olive capped Warbler and Western Spindalis. Lunch in town. Rest and visit the trail "Maravillas de Viñales", about 30 minutes from the town. This in an exceptional place to get photographs of Cuban Tody, La Sagra Flycatcher and Loggerhead Kingbird. You can get too, many winter migratory as diverse species of Warblers, Tanagers and Orioles.
In addition to photographing several species of snails and reptile’s endemic to Cuba, like the Cuban Stream Anole, the only aquatic habitat lizard in the Caribbean.

In Viñales we will visit Nils Navarro’s house. Nils is the most important ornithologist in Cuba at this time, and author of the Endemics Birds of Cuba: A Comprehensive Guide. Illustrated with his own watercolors. Author too of The Checklist of the Birds of Cuba, a periodical edition updated each year with many useful information and every new record of birds to Cuba. We will enjoy a talk about his upcoming new book, The Guide of Cuban Birds.

Accommodation: Guesthouse, Viñales
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 3 ~  Las Terrazas to Zapata Peninsula

Early departure to Sierra de Rosario Reserve. Morning birding in Soroa and Las Terrazas. Cuban Grassquit, Fernandina’s Flicker, Cuban Pewee, Giant Kingbird and Red-legged Thrush are likely species to encounter.
Lunch at Cafetal Buena Vista, a restored 19th century coffee plantation, offering sweeping views of the mountains, plains and coast. Departure east to the Zapata Peninsula (4 + hours).
Birding stops along the way if time allows.
Accommodation / dinner in private home stays in Playa Larga (3 nights in B&B style accommodations) on the picturesque Bay of Pigs. This region contains the largest wetland complex in the West Indies, and is Cuba’s most prolific birding region.
Here we have access to excellent local guides, as well as to protected areas and birding locations off the beaten track.

Accommodation: Guesthouses, Playa Larga (3 nights)
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner


Day 4 ~  Santo Tomas

Early breakfast and departure for birding within Zapata National Park and a morning boat ride in the waterchannels of Santo Tomas hamlet. This is our best opportunity for Zapata Wren, Zapata Sparrow, Red-shouldered Blackbird and and a variety of warblers and other migrants. Lunch, then afternoon visit to Casa Ana Birding to see the Bee Hummingbird (world’s smallest bird) along winter warblers.

Accommodation: Guesthouses, Playa Larga
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner


Day 5 ~  Zapata Peninsula

Breakfast and trip to Las Salinas Wildlife Refuge in the afternoon, with numerous shorebirds, Reddish Egrets, Wood Storks, Spoonbills, Flamingos and endemic Cuban Black Hawk. All walking is on dry, flat terrain (less than 2 miles total). Return to Playa larga. Lunch. Late afternoon walk in Soplillar Savannah for Cuban Parakeet, Cuban Parrot and Cuban Nightjar.

Accommodation: Guesthouses, Playa Larga
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 6 ~  Caribbean Coast

Early breakfast and departure to Bermejas. We arrive before sunrise t the hides, here is our best opportunity to see Blue-headed and Gray-fronted Quail-Doves, Key West and Ruddy Quail Doves. Also the endemic Fernandina’s Flicker, Bare-legged Owl and Cuban Blackbird. Continue to Cienfuegos town, with stops at Babiney rice fields for waterfowls such as King Rail, Spotted Rail and Glossy Ibis. Lunch in town. Visit Cienfuegos Botanical Garden, chances are we find a Gundlach Hawk active nest. Followed by a drive to Guajimico International Villa.

Accommodation: Villa Guajimico (1 night)
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 7 ~   Trinidad to the Atlantic Coast

Breakfast, check out. Our route is to the south and west through the scenic Escambray Valley in search of Palm Crow, and if previously missed, Giant Kingbird. The southern coast is also an area where Oriente Warbler and Cuban Gnatcatcher can be seen. Box lunch. Departure east and north towards Cayo Coco (5 + hours), the setting for Hemingway’s Islands in the Stream. The ‘keys’ offer excellent birding, scenery and variety of shorebirds.

Accommodation: All-Inclusive Hotel, Cayo Coco (2 nights)
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 8 ~   Cayo Coco

Morning Birding on Cayo Paredon Grande, the northeastern-most key in the archipelago, which is one of Cuba’s most important migratory landfalls. Cuban Gnatcatcher, Oriente Warbler, Zapata Sparrow ( local subs), the locally endangered Bahama Mockingbird and Thick-billed Vireo are target birds for the day. Lunch followed by afternoon birding on Cayo Guillermo in mixed
habitat including coastal forest, mangrove flats, low coastal scrub, and pristine white sand shoreline (roadside, flat walking). Shorebirds, aquatic, and wading birds abound, West-Indian Whistling Duck, Piping Plover, American Oystercatcher, White Ibis, and others.

Accommodation: All-Inclusive Hotel, Cayo Coco
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 9 ~  Havana

Morning birding session at Wild Boar cave for any missed target birds. Departure to the west towards Havana, with birding stops and lunch enroute (6 + hours).

Accommodation: Hostal Boutique, Havana ( 1 or 2* nights)
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Day 10  Havana - U.S.

Breakfast and transfer to Jose Marti International Airport for your return flight to the U.S.

Our very popular one day/night Havana extension includes breakfast, lunch, dinner, guide services, ground transportation, accommodation (same accommodation as the previous night), and a guided
walking exploration of Old Havana, a UNESCO World Heritage site and one of the most unique destinations in the Caribbean. Havana is loaded with interesting historic sites, museums, art galleries, and now several excellent restaurants. Due a half century of relative economic isolation, Havana avoided rapid development and the loss of scores of historic residential and commercial properties during  the last half of the 20th century. Many buildings have been recently refurbished, some dating back to the 17th century. The harbor in Old Havana is presided over by Fortaleza de San Carlos de la Cabana, which is among the largest and best preserved Spanish Colonial fortresses in the world. The Malecon, Havana’s historic and scenic ocean drive, winds several miles along the coast from the harbor in Old Havana to the mouth of the Almendares River,
