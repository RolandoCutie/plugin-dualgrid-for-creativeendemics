HERP-watching IN EASTERN CUBA ( 14 days )
PRICE 3250 USD + 500 SGL
Join Wild Endemics on a 14‑day herpetology expedition through Eastern Cuba, the island’s most biodiverse region. From semi‑deserts to rainforests, discover frogs, lizards, snakes, and mollusks found nowhere else on Earth. Alongside fieldwork, immerse yourself in Cuban culture, history, and conservation stories.
Highlights of the Trip
- Gibara – coastal caves, endemic anoles, and cultural heritage
- Cabo Cruz – limestone forests, false chameleons, rainbow boas and night frogwatching
- Gran Piedra – cool mountain habitats with diverse frog communities and dwarf boas.
- Guantánamo Bay – semidesert trails with iguanas, cacti ecosystems, and geckos.
- Baitiquirí & Imias – desert reptiles, rock iguanas,  and local endemics.
- Baracoa & Yunque – rainforest hikes, cacao farms, painted tree snails and rare amphibians.
- Humboldt National Park – home of the Monte Iberia dwarf frog, one of the world’s smallest.
- Pinares de Mayarí – diverse ecosystems, orchids, waterfalls, and night herping.
- Salto del Guayabo – dramatic waterfalls, lush flora, and endemic twig anoles.

Overview
Wild Endemics invites you on a small-group herpetology and natural history expedition through Cuba’s most biodiverse  region, Eastern Cuba (El Oriente). Cuba’s herpetofauna is extraordinary: 68 frog and toad species (95% endemic) and 153 reptiles (88% endemic).
An invitation to explore not only the reptile species of the eastern region of the island of Cuba, full of endemism and unique values, but also to witness in real time the evolutionary processes on a small scale, where it will be possible to observe a whole range of subspecific variations between species, as well as the existence of geographically and ecologically isolated twin species. We will go from semi-desert habitats to tropical rainforest areas in just a few minutes, observing the wonderful landscapes and admiring the most colorful terrestrial mollusks on the planet (Polymita picta) and lots of endemic birds. We combine nature and local culture in a harmonious way to make your trip an unforgettable experience.
Beyond the fieldwork, our Cuban naturalist guide will facilitate candid conversations on Cuba’s conservation challenges and daily life. Expect informed insights into the nation’s history, healthcare, education, housing, and transport systems, providing a richer context for your wildlife observations and cultural encounters.

ITINERARY
DAY 1 Arrival
Arrival to Frank Pais International Holguin airport in American Airlines at noon, with chances of seeing the first Cuban reptiles such the colorful Cuban Blue Anole (Anolis allisoni) and the Cuban Brown Anole (Anolis sagrei) in the airport. Transfer to Gibara town (1h+), in the north Atlantic coast of Holguin. Check in Hotel Ordoño in Gibara. Welcome cocktail. Short walt city tour, Gibara is a charming fishermen village with old defensive walls from Colonial times, and which still has some remains, discovering the most important cultural aspects, just as the high number of myths and stories linked to the city. We will stop at the main landmarks, like Colonial Fine Art Museum, Central Park, Fortress, and most importantly, the Gibara Natural History Museum, with a large reptile, mollusks and bird collection. Welcome dinner at the hotel terrace.

DAY 2 Gibara Coast & Caves Bakers Cave in Gibara
Breakfast at the hotel. Explore “Cueva de los Panaderos” (Bakers' Cave ) in Gibara, it is a cave system that shelters native fauna plenty on troglobitic local endemic species such a Blind Criket (Cubacophus gibarensis), with magnificent formations of stalactites and stalagmites also is the habitat for: Cuban Cave Frog (Eleutheroctylus thomasi), Cuban Groinspot Frog (E. atkinsi), Greenhouse frog (E.planirostris), American wall gecko (Tarentola americana) and see the communal nests of Cave Anole (Anolis Lucius). Baker’s Cave is the type locality of the recently new species described Gibara white-collared racerlet (Arrhyton albicollum), on the trail will be possible to find Cuban Coast Anole (Anolis jubar gibarensis), Cave Anole (Anolis lucius), Cuban trunk anole (A. argenteolus), Cuban twig anole (Anolis angusticeps), Cuban curlytail lizard (Leiocephalus cubensis), Black-spotted least gecko (Sphaerodactylus nigropunctatus), Cuban racer (Cubophis cantherigerus schwartzi), Cuban boa (Chilabothrus angulifer). Lunch with sea view. Continue to Las Balsas Wetland, and tour along the coast to Caletones to observe the local endemic subspecies: Pallid curlytail lizard (L. raviceps delavarai), Cuban striped curlytail (L. stictigaster gibarensis), Cuban side-blotched curlytail (L. macropus lenticulatus), Cuban ameiva (P. auberi citra), Central Anole (A. centrales). Return to Gibara. Dinner in a Cuban restuarant overlooking the town and with seaview. Night herping at “Cueva de los Panaderos”.
DAY 3 Cupeycillo Valley
Breakfast. Mornnig trails in Cupeycillo Valley near Gibara. Targets Cupeicillo: Knight anole (Anolis equestris thomasi), Cuban trunk anole (A. argenteolus), A. centralis, Anolis homolechis, Anolis allisoni x porcatus (introgression zone), Anolis porcus (red-fanned) prob. new species). Lunch in Holguin town. Continue to Playa Las Coloradas in Granma (4+). During the trip we will border the north slope of the Sierra Maestra. Dinner. Accommodation in B&B. 2 nights.
DAY 4 Cabo Cruz
Full day herpwatching in Cabo Cruz. Visit to the trail El Guafe in Cabo Cruz. The mean habitat are evergreen and deciduous forests that grows on limestone ground. We will enjoy local endemics like the common Cabo Cruz Banded Anole (Anolis guafe), the rare Cabo Cruz False Chamaeleon (Chamaeleolis Anolis agueroi), the local endemic subspecies of Cuban Giant Anole (Anolis equestris verreonensis), and the Cabo Cruz Pallid Anole (Anolis ruibali), Anolis guafe and Anolis confusus. Lunch in Cabo Cruz. Continue herping in the afternoon. Dinner in Playa Las Coloradas. At night we will come back for frogs, such as the local endemic Cabo Cruz Frog (Eleutherodactylus tonyi), Eastern Giant Toads (Bufo peltocephalus), the Cuban Groin Spot Frog (E. atkinsi), a very rare population of the Bromeliad Frog (E. varians). We will start trying to find the first Cuban Night Lizards(Cricosaura typica), but will have enough time the following day. Accommodation in B&B.

DAY 5 Playa Las Coloradas
In route to Cabo Cruz. We will have more opportunities to see many of the species mentioned above, and also the locally endemic Cabo Cruz Trunk Anole (Anolis confusus) and the Cuban Tree Frog (Osteopilus septentrionalis). The Cuban Three-banded Sphaero (Sphaerodactylus nigropunctatus ocujal) is a beautifully colored gecko, with banded females and spotted males on a yellow background. Lunch. Departure to Santiago (5+). Dinner in Santiago. Accomodation in Villa Gran Piedra 2 nights.

DAY 6 Gran Piedra
Breakfast. Full day in the Sierra de la Gran Piedra, in the eastern portion of Sierra Maestra, has 1125 m above the sea level. After the high lowland temperatures, we will enjoy the cool enviroment of the mountain. It is a very different habitat with one of the biggest frog communities in the island. It will be possible to see: the Oriente Yellow Mottled Frog (Eleutherodactylus ricordii), the Oriente Spiny Frog (E. gundlachi), the Yellow Striped Dwarf Frog (E. limbatus), the Oriente Dark Bellied Frog (E. intermedius), the Guantanamera Frog (E. guantanamera) (easier to find inside the bromelias compared with other localities), the Cuban Grass Frog (E. varleyi), the Cuban Telegraph Frog (E. auriculatus), and others. Reptiles are represented by the local endemic Santiago Grass Anole (Anolis rejectus) and Anolis alutaceus, which is relatively easy to find. Other species are the Spanish Flag Anole (Anolis allogus), the rare Cuban Forest Sphaero (Sphaerodactylus ramsdeni), and the Oriente Giant Anole (Anolis noblei galeifer). Lunch in La Gran Piedra. Afternoon visit to the exotic garden Flor Ave del Paraiso. Dinner. Night herpwatch.
DAY 7 Guantanamo Bay
Breakfast in the hotel. In route to the semidesertic south coast of Guantánamo Province. In about 2 hours we will be arriving to the sorroundings of Guantánamo Bay. Los Monitongos Trail. There is a captivating herpetofauna associated to the dry scrub vegetation of the south coast of
Guantánamo (which also includes tall column cacti) and Melocactus (Melocactus evae) growing
on marine terraces, which include Anolis jubar albershwartsi (probably raised to specific level);
Anolis smallwoodi palardis, Anolis litoralis, Leiocephalus raviceps raviceps and Cuban Rock Iguana (Cyclura nubila), limestone walls, and typical small outcrops of volcanic rock called "monitongos". Please, be prepared for high temperatures but awesome landscapes!. A very interesting local endemic is the Imias Rocky Anole (Anolis imias), an endangered species that was long time hard to find due to the generalized ignorance ofits life habits. The semidesertic region is also the habitat of nice colored geckoes of the genus Sphaerodactylus, such as the Guantánamo Bay Sphaero (S. ruibali), and the Cuban Broad Banded Sphaero (S. torrei spielmani). We will take advantage to visit the protected area of Hatibonico and also, by road, some marine terraces. The small Crombie´s Tarentola (Tarentola crombiei) is a recently described species that occurs together with the Cuban Giant Gecko (Tarentola americana). To find Tarentola and Sphaeros we will turn out dry plants of the genus Agave, for which it is important to protect hands. Return to Guantanamo. Accommodation in Hotel Marti 1 night.
DAY 8 Baitiquirí
Breakfast in the hotel. Departure to Baitiquirí (1h). Along the trip we will have
very nice views of the semidesertic south coast of Guantánamno. We will stop in the way to take pictures and for reptiles like Cuban Rock Iguana, Anolis imias, Anolis jubar, Anolis litoralis, and Sphaerodactylus torrei spielmani and S. ruibali). Imias Rocky Anole is the main target and a very interesting local endemic, an endangered species that was long time hard to find due to the generalized unknown habits. Along our tour we will see the subspecific variation of the Cuban Coast Anoles (Anolis jubar sspp.). Other species are the Oriente Pallid Anole (Anolis litoralis), Cuban Ameiva (Pholidoscelis auberi), including several subspecies), racers, the rare Oriente Browncapped Ground Snake (Arrhyton redimitum), the Gracile Banded Trope (Tropidophis wrighti), the wonderful Green Blotched Giant Anole (Anolis smallwoodi palardis), the Pallid Curly Tail (Leiocephalus raviceps), and many others. Amphibians are poorly represented, but the locally endemic Oriente Coastal Frog (Eleutherodactylus etheridgei) is very well adapted to survive in the desert conditions. Lunch in Baitiquiri Flora and Fauna facility. Continue to Baracoa, with a couple of stops in “La Farola”, aka ‘Cuban Alps’, a stunningly scenic mountain road. This mountain range includes both mesic and arid zone habitat. In essence, this drive takes us from Cuba’s dryest region to the wettest in couple of hours. Arrival to Baracoa. Accommodation in Porto Santo Hotel 3 nights. Dinner.
DAY 9 Yumuri River Canyon
Breakfast followed by drive to Yumuri canyon. We will take a boat ride along the river to the
trail and continue walking under the exuberant vegetation and clear water of the river with the
possibility to see many lizards (Anolis allogus, Anolis homolechis, Anolis jubar maisiensis, Anolis baracoae, Diplosssus nigropunctatus, Anolis argillaceus, Anolis porcatus, Anolis alutaceus), thropes and other local frogs, specially the Painted tree Snail (Polymita picta) the most colorful land snail of the world.Lunch at the local paladar located on the beach area with a traditional Cuban food. After lunchwe will visit a private Cacao farm run by women. Where we will learn how the chocolate is made, all the process from the plantations to the final product, everything manually made, and tasting of local products. Return to Baracoa. DAY 10 Yunque de Baracoa
Will visit the surroundings of “El Yunque”, a very famous flat top mountain that first captivated Cristopher Columbus when arriving to Cuba. Very close to the base of “El Yunque” is the Duaba River, with clean waters. The locality is covered by rainforests. It takes one and halfhour to climb up to the mountain with a local guide, which also requires to cross the river at a point. There are several species of amphibians and reptiles in this locality, such as the hard to see but easy to hear Guantanamera Frog (E. guantanamera), the very common Oriente Mottled Frog (E.simulans), the Cuban Khaki Frog (E. ronaldi), and two local beautiful endemics which are possible to find climbing up to 300 m: the Cuban Redrumped Frog (E. acmonis) and the Baracoa Dwarf Frog (E. orientalis). There is also an acoustic variant of the Cuban Telegraph Frog (E. auriculatus) that may represent an undescribed cryptic species. The Cuban Spotted Toad (Peltophryne taladai) is also very common in this place, and use to breed at the edges ofthe Duaba River. The Cuban Tree Frog (Osteopilus septentrionalis) is everywhere. Lizards are represented by the Baracoa Giant Anole (Anolis baracoae), the beautiful and common Green Fern Anole (A. cyanopleurus), the Blue Eyed Twig Anole (A. alutaceus), the hard to see Guantanamo Twig Anole (Anolis alayoni), the frequent Cuban Side-blotched Curly Tail (Leiocephalus macropus asbolomus), the difficult to see Oriente Bearded False Chamaeleon (Yellowfanned) (Chamaeleolis porcus), and many others. Snakes are not very common, but it is possible to see the Cuban Lesser Racer (Caraiba andreai orientalis), the Cuban Racer (Cubophis cantherigerus pepei), and the Giant Trope (Tropidophis melanurus). The Oriente Black Ground Snake (Arrhyton supernum) is a very hard to find local endemic, only known from few individuals. Also rare is the Cuban Spotted Galliwasp (Diploglossus nigropunctatus). Because “El Yunque” is just few kilometers far away from the Hotel, the option is to spent the afternoon and early night looking for herps. Accommodation in Porto Santo Hotel. Dinner.
DAY 11 Humboldt National Park
Full day herp-watching in Humboldt National Park, 40 km away from Baracoa. Today the star is
the Monte Iberia Dwarf Frog (Eleutherodactylus iberia), one of the smallest frogs in the World, but also Eleutherodactylus toa, E. dimidiatus, E. riparius, and much more. We will look for this tiny frog in the leaflitter of the tropical forest, where the targets will be: Anolis rubribarbus, Anolis isolepis, Anolis inexpectatus, Anolis ohiolepis, Anolis baracoae, Anolis fugitivus, Anolis argillaceus, Anolis alayoni, Anolis homolechis, Anolis allogus. Trough the trails crossing tropical
forests, there is a second chance to see many endemic birds as well. Lunch in Finca Indio. Continue to Pinares de Mayari (4h+). Accommodation in Villa Pinares 2 nights.
DAY 1 2 Pinares de Mayari

in a waterfall. The trail has 8 different ecosystems characterized by their diversity and fragility. Also, there is a beautiful orchid area with most of the existing species in the region. Lunch in the Villa. Afternoon Trail La Sabina. Dinner. Night herpwatching. Targets are: Anolis anfiloquioi, Anolis noblei noblei, Anolis cupeyalensis, Anolis argentteolus, Anolis porcatus, Anolis ophiolepis,
Tropidophis wrighti, Tropidophis melanurus, Caraiba andrai, Cubophis canterigerus pepei, among others.
DAY 1 3 Salto del Guayabo
After breakfast, visit Salto del Guayabo (Guayabo Falls), which it is the main attraction in Mensura National Park. The waters of Guayabo River, 546 m above sea-level, flow loudly in two vertical falls, the first a fall of 85 and the second of 127 meters. The waterfall has two trails as well, the first takes you all the way to the waterfall top and where there is a semi-natural pool. And a larger trail that goes all the way to the bottom and base of the waterfalls. The place, apart from offering you an astonishing view towards the waterfall, is improved by its surroundings. Large variety of flora and fauna, among which we emphasize the range of ferns, flowers, trees and birds. Lunch is at a comfortable look out point 700m feet above sea level, with tremendous views of the Falls. Tragets: Anolis anfiloquioi, Anolis noblei noblei, Anolis cupeyalensis, Anolis argentteolus, Anolis porcatus, Anolis ophiolepis, Leiocephalus macropus asbolomus, Tropidophis melanurus, Caraiba andrai, Cubophis canterigerus pepei, among others. Continue to Holguin (3+). Accommodation in Hotel Mystique Saratoga. Farewell Dinner in 1910 Restaurant.
