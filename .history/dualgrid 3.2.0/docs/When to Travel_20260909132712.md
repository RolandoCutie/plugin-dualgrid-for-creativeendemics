When to Travel
What’s the best season for my trip to Cuba?
Cuba welcomes you with its tropical climate, rich traditions, and remarkable biodiversity. It’s one of the safest countries in Latin America, where colonial architecture and natural landscapes feel like pages from a photography book. With such contrasts, Cuba stands out for its high level of endemism — unique species found nowhere else.
Cuba’s tropical climate, remarkable biodiversity, safety, natural landscape and traditions, makes it a year round destination, but the best months vary by activity:
The prime months are November through April,
Avoiding summer: June to September coincides with the hurricane season and high temperatures, which can disrupt travel.

Nature & Culture Trips – available year round, combining wildlife encounters with heritage and traditions.
Birding – November through April, with February and March offering peak overlap of migratory and resident species, spectacular courtship displays, and longer checklists.
Hiking – best enjoyed in the cooler, drier months from November to April.
Herping – June is ideal, when rains spark activity in frogs and lizards. Hurricane risk is low, making it the safest month for reptile and amphibian observation.
Snail Expeditions – late June to September, coinciding with peak activity of Cuba’s diverse mollusk fauna.
Where We Travel
Our expeditions span Cuba’s most remarkable ecological regions, chosen for their biodiversity, scenery, and cultural value. Whether your focus is birds, reptiles, amphibians, snails, hiking, or photography, these destinations offer a complete immersion into Cuba’s natural heritage. Guests visit national parks, biosphere reserves, and unique habitats, often accompanied by Cuban scientists and conservationists.
Western Mountains (Sierra del Rosario & Sierra de los Órganos): Submontane rainforests, pine forests, and karst landscapes with mogotes — towering limestone monoliths. Rich in endemics and iconic rural scenery.
Zapata Swamp: The Caribbean’s largest wetland, a Ramsar site with cenotes, coral reefs, and savannahs. Home to rare birds, reptiles, and the Cuban Crocodile breeding center.
Escambray Mountains & Caribbean Coast: Scenic valleys and foothills with cooler climate, diverse flora and fauna, and rewarding hikes.
Atlantic Archipelago (Cayo Coco & keys): Barrier islands linked by a 36 km causeway, offering prime habitats for shorebirds, reptiles, and coastal ecosystems.
Guanahacabibes Peninsula: A UNESCO Biosphere Reserve at Cuba’s western tip, known for migratory corridors, pristine forests, and nesting sea turtles.
Gran Piedra: A volcanic massif in Santiago de Cuba, surrounded by montane forests rich in orchids, butterflies, and endemic wildlife.
Alejandro de Humboldt National Park: UNESCO World Heritage Site, one of the world’s most biologically diverse tropical island parks, with exceptional endemism.
Sierra Maestra: Cuba’s largest mountain range, combining historical significance with rugged habitats for raptors, reptiles, and montane flora.
Pinares de Mayarí: Highland pine forests in Holguín province, home to Cuban Solitaire, local orchids and unique pine forests.
Sierra de Najasa (Camagüey): Semi deciduous forests and limestone hills, famous for Giant Kingbird and Palm Crow population.
Guantánamo South Coast: Rugged coastal landscapes with dry forests and Caribbean vistas, supporting Oriente Warbler, Cuban Gnatcatcher, and other several reptiles.
