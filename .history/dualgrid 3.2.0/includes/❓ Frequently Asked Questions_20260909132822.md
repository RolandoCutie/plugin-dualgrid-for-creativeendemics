❓ Frequently Asked Questions
Arrival & Departure
Where do tours start and end? Most expeditions begin at José Martí International Airport in Havana (HAV), while in eastern Cuba the chosen airports are Frank País International Airport in Holguín (HOG) or Antonio Maceo in Santiago de Cuba ( SCU)depending on the itinerary. Airport transfers are included.
Can I arrive earlier or stay longer? Yes, extensions in Havana and Varadero can be arranged.
Accommodation
What type of lodging is provided? Comfortable casas particulares (B&Bs), boutique hostals, and selected hotels.
Are rooms private? Yes, with the option of a single supplement for solo travelers.
Meals & Food
Are meals included? Three meals per day are provided, starting with dinner on Day 1 through breakfast on the final day.
Can dietary needs be accommodated? Yes — vegetarian, vegan, and light spice preferences can be arranged with advance notice.
Wildlife & Focus
What species will I see? Depending on the tour: Cuban endemics (birds, reptiles, amphibians, snails), Caribbean specialties, orchids, and butterflies.
Are sightings guaranteed? No, but itineraries are designed around peak habitats and seasons to maximize encounters.
Physical Demands
How difficult are the hikes? Most walks are gentle to moderate, unless specified in the trekking itineraries, with occasional hills or uneven terrain.
Do I need to be very fit? General good health is sufficient; itineraries include rest breaks and midday pauses.
Costs & Payments
What’s included in the price? Lodging, meals, guides, transportation, trail fees, and conservation contributions.
What’s not included? International airfare, Cuban visa (~$100), insureancegratuities, and personal expenses.

Conservation & Community
How does my trip support conservation? Each expedition contributes to local conservation projects and encourages guests to bring small donations (school supplies, medicines, rechargeable lamps).
Will I meet local scientists? Yes — tours often include talks with Cuban specialists and conservation partners.

Cash & Payments
Can I use credit cards in Cuba? International cards (Visa/Mastercard) often do not work due to U.S. restrictions. It’s best to bring cash in usd, euros, Canadian dollars, or pounds.
Are ATMs reliable? ATMs are limited and may not accept foreign cards. Carrying cash is strongly recommended.
How much cash should I bring? We estimate you will need between $200 and $400 USD for local expenses during the tour, such drinks, souvenirs, gratuities and optional activities.

Water Safety
Is tap water safe to drink? Tap water is not recommended. Bottled water is widely available and provided during tours.
Can I refill bottles? Yes, purified water is available at hotels and eco lodges.
U.S. Citizens Traveling to Cuba
Can Americans visit Cuba? Yes, but U.S. citizens must travel under one of the 12 authorized categories (most birding and nature tours qualify under “Support for the Cuban People”).
Do I need special paperwork? Once you book your trip, we will provide the required information to comply with U.S regulations.
Visa Requirements
Do I need a visa to enter Cuba? Yes, all travelers require a Cuban Tourist Card (eVisa). This is usually arranged through airlines or tour operators before arrival and can be easily purchase on-line.
How much does it cost? Approximately $90 USD, depending on the issuing airline or agency.
Internet Connection
Is Wi Fi available? Internet access is improving but still limited. Wi Fi is available at hotels, casas particulares, and public hotspots using prepaid cards.
How reliable is it? Speeds are slower than international standards, and coverage can be patchy outside major cities.
